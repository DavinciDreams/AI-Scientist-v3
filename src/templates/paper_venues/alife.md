# ALIFE 2026 Venue Brief

Use this brief to tailor the manuscript for the Artificial Life Conference 2026 submission.
The official conference theme is "Living and Lifelike Complex Adaptive Systems."

## Hard Constraints
- Target venue: ALIFE 2026.
- Submission type: full paper.
- Body-page budget: {{PAPER_PAGE_BUDGET}}
- Review model: single-blind. Include real author names and affiliations in the manuscript.
- The paper must satisfy the ALIFE template and formatting rules selected by `--paper-format {{PAPER_FORMAT}}`.

## Audience
- Artificial life researchers, complex systems researchers, computational neuroscience adjacent readers, and ML researchers interested in adaptive or life-like computation.
- Readers are likely to value mechanistic interpretation, adaptive dynamics, emergent behavior, robustness, and conceptual relevance beyond raw benchmark gains.

## ALIFE Framing Priorities
- Lead with the paper's Artificial Life question, not only its benchmark result.
- Frame the contribution as evidence about adaptive computation, learning dynamics, error-driven self-organization, or life-like information processing when the grounded artifacts support that framing.
- Connect empirical results to broader ALIFE questions such as adaptation, robustness, distributed coordination, emergence, open-ended variation, or biologically inspired learning.
- Emphasize what the studied system teaches us, not only that it scores better.
- Keep the paper scientifically modest: grounded mechanism, grounded comparisons, explicit limits.

## CFP-Derived Topic Anchors
- Predictive Methods for Complex Adaptive Systems and Life-like Systems
- Multiscale Emergence, Robustness and Plasticity
- Constructive Dynamical Systems and Complexity
- Self-organization, Swarms, Stigmergy
- Information-Theoretic Methods in Lifelike Systems
- Phenotypic Plasticity and Adaptability in Scalable, Robust Growing Systems

## Manuscript Guidance
- Prefer titles and abstracts that sound like an ALIFE paper, not a generic benchmark-optimization report.
- In the title, abstract, introduction, and conclusion, include at least one reader-facing connection to Artificial Life, life-like systems, adaptive computation, or biologically plausible learning when grounded.
- In the introduction and conclusion, explain why the method or phenomenon is relevant to adaptive or life-like systems.
- In the method section, highlight the architectural or dynamical ingredients that matter for the claimed behavior.
- In the results section, emphasize behavior patterns, stability, failure modes, and cross-setting consistency when grounded.
- Figures must meet top-tier conference standards in both design (clean typography, consistent colors, proper labels/legends, high resolution) and scientific content (clear message, no filler).
- The template is two-column: default to single-column figures; use full-width (`figure*`) only when single-column would be unreadable.

## Anti-Goals
- Do not present the work as only an engineering search story.
- Do not lead with benchmark optimization, transfer performance, or architecture scaling alone if the grounded evidence supports a stronger ALIFE framing.
- Do not over-index on generic ML benchmarking language if the grounded evidence supports a richer ALIFE interpretation.
- Do not drift into deployment, systems, edge-device, or generic application narratives unless that story is central and grounded.
- Do not claim biological plausibility, emergence, open-endedness, or generality unless those claims are directly supported by the artifacts.
- Do not force ALIFE buzzwords into the paper when the evidence only supports a narrower empirical claim.

## Decision Rule
- When venue framing and grounded evidence conflict, follow the grounded evidence.
- Use this brief to choose emphasis, terminology, and narrative framing, not to invent stronger claims than the run supports.
